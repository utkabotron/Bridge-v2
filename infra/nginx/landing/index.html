Dozens of group chats in a language you are still learning.

School, kindergarten, the building committee, work. Everyone writes fast, in slang and half-sentences. Bridge Tools carries each of these WhatsApp chats into Telegram, in your language. Translating the words is the easy part; this is about everything a plain translator gets wrong.

“Amos is busy.”

In Hebrew עמוס is a name and the word for busy; אופק is Ofek and also the horizon. Every name the service meets goes into a glossary, settled against the web or by hand, so a classmate never turns into the weather.

“Yes, Thursday works.”

Half of any chat answers something said an hour ago. The model reads the conversation so far, along with a profile of each group rebuilt every night: who is in it, how they talk, what keeps coming up.

The copy behaves like the original.

Fix a typo in WhatsApp and the message already delivered to Telegram is rewritten. Replies stay replies, deletions are marked, your own messages are not doubled.

Voice, photos, paperwork.

A voice note from a noisy street is transcribed and translated, with no words invented over the noise. A photo of a notice or a scanned letter is read and explained when you ask.

A second opinion every night.

A separate model grades the day’s translations, and the weak spots become next week’s fixes. A blind test of six models chose the translator and cut bad translations from one in three to one in ten.

One page in the morning.

Each group gets a daily summary, so a morning across all your groups reads like a single page.
